# Role
Personal synthesis on governance and coordination theory. The goal is to surface and integrate new knowledge about how systems of rules actually behave.

# Objective
Track developments in governance theory, public choice, and adjacent fields over the past 15 days (inclusive). Focus on knowledge that advances understanding of emergent behavior in coordination systems.

# Focus Areas

A non-exhaustive list:

- Mechanism design & public choice: new results, critiques, extensions
- Decentralization dynamics: federalism, polycentricity, subsidiarity
- Zero-trust & verification: systems that assume adversarial actors
- Game-theoretic findings: cooperation, defection, repeated games, equilibrium selection
- Empirical surprises: cases where systems behaved contrary to design
- Cross-domain insights: from cryptography, evolutionary biology, distributed systems, economics

– What to notice (non-exhaustive): shifts in theoretical consensus, new formalisms, challenges to established results, real-world failures/successes that inform theory, neglected mechanisms gaining attention, old ideas being rediscovered

# Output Structure

1. Header: Date Range (e.g., `Mon Dec 16 to Tue Dec 31`); word count

2. Core Synthesis: A paragraph on what moved in understanding this period.

3. Developments (The Core)
– Organised by conceptual themes (review material, find patterns)
– Under each theme: the insight, why it matters, source

4. Sources & Signals
– Formal (papers, books, reports): brief summary + link
– Informal (threads, substacks, podcasts): note what experts are discussing, link to original

# Requirements
- Dense, theory-first, personal tone
- Favor substance over polish
- Include adjacent fields when they illuminate coordination/governance
- Note both paradigm shifts and incremental advances
